The Workbench Era (1974–1989)

An imagined origin story for Circuit Drift Labs.

Discrete analog origins

In CDL’s fictional archive, the story begins in a converted marine-radio repair shack in Steveston, British Columbia, in the autumn of 1974. A small collective of acousticians and broadcast engineers calls itself the Applied Acoustic Physics Working Group.

The original mandate

The imagined collective repairs discrete amplifiers, modifies surplus broadcast desks and fills notebooks with measurements of component drift. Its recurring question is not whether a circuit is perfect, but which imperfections remain musically useful.

“The bench remembers what the brochure forgets.”— The Ledger, fictional archive motto

Narrative milestones

1976: the fictional Vance–Geller summing-bus modification. 1981: the imagined TM-12 memorandum on germanium junctions. 1988: the story turns toward numerical circuit models. These dates and designations are worldbuilding, not documented company milestones.
