# The Workbench Era (1974–1989)

**Fictional CDL lore / not company history**

An imagined origin story for Circuit Drift Labs.

<section><h2>Discrete analog origins</h2><p>In CDL’s fictional archive, the story begins in a converted marine-radio repair shack in Steveston, British Columbia, in the autumn of 1974. A small collective of acousticians and broadcast engineers calls itself the Applied Acoustic Physics Working Group.</p></section><section><h2>The original mandate</h2><p>The imagined collective repairs discrete amplifiers, modifies surplus broadcast desks and fills notebooks with measurements of component drift. Its recurring question is not whether a circuit is perfect, but which imperfections remain musically useful.</p></section><blockquote>“The bench remembers what the brochure forgets.”<cite>— The Ledger, fictional archive motto</cite></blockquote><section><h2>Narrative milestones</h2><p>1976: the fictional Vance–Geller summing-bus modification. 1981: the imagined TM-12 memorandum on germanium junctions. 1988: the story turns toward numerical circuit models. These dates and designations are worldbuilding, not documented company milestones.</p></section>
